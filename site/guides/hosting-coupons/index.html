Check the offer before paying

1. Match the product. Identify whether the offer names shared hosting, VPS, a domain, email, or an account credit. A brand name alone is not eligibility.

2. Identify the delivery method. Check whether the official page prints a code, sends you through a campaign link, or says the credit appears automatically. A code field in a cart does not prove a code applies.

3. Read the whole payment path. Keep the initial payable total, required term, renewal amount, taxes and offer end date beside the code or campaign claim. Check the actual account and checkout before relying on any public-page figure.

Why a promotions page can send you to the wrong product

Namecheap's September monthly coupon page lists codes for domain registration and transfer, professional email, and SSL. Its separate hosting campaign explicitly says no code is needed. Both were read on September 24, 2026. A code's presence on a brand's coupon page therefore does not establish that it applies to that brand's hosting checkout.

FAQ

Do I always need a code for a hosting discount?

No. Namecheap's dated birthday campaign says the qualifying discount applies automatically. Hostinger's coupon page shows a code on selected hosting plans. Check the relevant official product page rather than assuming one method fits every offer.

Is a signup credit the same as a lower hosting rate?

No. DigitalOcean describes a balance applied automatically to a first team; it does not describe that credit as a reduction to a named plan's published price.

Does a coupon on a brand's website work at renewal?

Not necessarily. Namecheap's cited hosting campaign explicitly excludes renewals. A different offer needs its own renewal terms, and the final account or cart total still needs checking.
