CLOUDWAYS ARCHIVE RECORD

Cloudways coupon archive: what Cloudways’ own pages printed, 2018 to 2022

Short answer: Cloudways’ own pages printed BFCM18 with 30% off for 3 months in 2018, BFCM40 with 40% off for 3 months and a December 4, 2019 deadline in 2019, BFCM2021 with 40% off for 4 months plus an advertised $10 hosting credit in 2021, and BFCM4030 with 40% off for 4 months plus up to 30 free migrations in 2022. Every row in the table below comes from a Cloudways-owned page and names the snapshot it was read from.

These are archived records of what the pages said, not current offers, and this page does not judge whether any code works today. For the present state of the official promo page, read the Cloudways coupon guide.

What the saved responses add

The stored coupon counter does not establish remaining stock

The December 2022 saved response contains Promo Code 0000 300 Total Coupons in its markup, alongside counter attributes. That is an initial HTML value, not an observed account checkout or a measured remaining balance. These values do not establish how many coupons remained redeemable.

The same initial countdown text appears in three saved responses

30 Days 18 Hours 54 Mins 09 Secs appears in the stored 2021-11-29, 2022-08-10 and 2022-12-03 HTML responses. Those responses also retain the date-picker values Oct 27, 2021 00:00:01 and Nov 29, 2021 18:59:59. This establishes what the saved HTML contained, not how JavaScript behaved in a visitor’s browser or how much time an offer had left.

The FAQ and the terms on one page disagree

In the 2022-12-03 response, a FAQ says the offer applies to users who sign up and upgrade from trial to a full account, while the terms say it is “only valid for new customers”. Both sentences sit in the same file. We report both statements because the source contradicts itself, and neither statement proves that an existing paid account qualified.

The same saved terms allow one coupon per claimant, while the FAQ says one use per order. Those are historical statements in the December 2022 source, not a current stacking or checkout test.

Some codes with “cloudways” in the name belong to other companies

In Cloudways’ saved roundup, CloudWaysFriday (Visual Modo), Cloudways@StoreYa (Coupon Pop), Cloudways (Mycred), CLOUDWAYS (Envira Gallery), CLOUDWAYS-20 (Cart2Cart), cloudways-BFCM (MexBS) and cloudwaysrender (Renderforest) are partner products’ codes, each under its named issuer. BFCM18 also appears under Inspectlet, Tyche Software and Profile Press; BFCM40 appears under WPDeveloper (NotificationX) in the 2019 roundup. The code string alone does not identify a Cloudways hosting offer; the product and issuer beside it do.

The address printed on the old pages

The 2018 and 2019 footers print 52 Springvale, Pope Pius XII Street, Mosta MST2653, Malta. The 2021 and 2022 footers print Junction Business Centre, 1st Floor, Sqaq Lourdes, St Julians STJ3334, Malta. A page in the limited September 21, 2026 comparison was recorded as printing Junction Business Centre, 1st Floor Sqaq Lourdes, St Julians, Mosta STJ 3334, Malta. We record those dated wordings side by side; this does not establish when an address changed.

How this archive was checked

These records come from saved Common Crawl responses of Cloudways-owned pages, not from today’s live pages. The dates in the table identify the snapshots. We rechecked the saved files on September 23, 2026; that was a local archive review, not a new checkout or live-offer test.

The comparison against search results was made on September 21, 2026. Nine of the ten result pages opened; one Reddit result could not be read because of a network-security block, so it is recorded as unread and is not used as evidence of absence. Two codes did appear in that set: DevOpsCube listed BFCM4030 and SUMMER306 as expired, and a further page listed SUMMER404. This archive documents the 2018 and 2019 rows and the source-level findings above without claiming they were absent elsewhere. The 2018 “new account” limit rests on a single source: it appears in one of the two Cloudways posts from that year, not both. This was a limited, dated comparison, not a claim that these findings are unique across the web.
